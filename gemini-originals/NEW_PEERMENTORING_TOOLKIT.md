 # Peer Mentoring Toolkit

 **Pilot-ready guidance for first-year belonging, low-threshold peer support and referral within Odisee Brussels' first-year support pathway**

 **ODISEE BRUSSELS - STUDENT SUCCESS PROJECT 2025-2026**

 ## Toolkit at a Glance
 Peer connection > Check-in > Support conversation > One small next step > Navigation or referral > Follow-up

 ### Purpose of this deliverable
 1. Strengthen first-year students' sense of belonging through low-threshold peer contact.
 2. Structure Odisee's existing buddy or peer-support practices without replacing them.
 3. Help peer mentors guide students toward appropriate support when ordinary peer support is not enough.

 ### Positioning Note
 This Peer Mentoring Toolkit translates the peer mentoring deliverable described in the case study into a pilot-ready, low-burden tool for Odisee Brussels. It is designed to help second-year or senior students support first-year students through approachable check-ins, shared transition experience, role clarity and referral guidance.

 The toolkit does not create a new large-scale peer mentoring programme from scratch. It also does not replace Odisee's existing buddy, peer-support or student support practices. Instead, it structures and strengthens what may already exist by clarifying the peer mentor role, offering check-in prompts, setting boundaries and connecting peer support to the wider first-year support chain.

 The toolkit is intended for a small pilot in one or two first-year programmes before wider use. It is deliberately adaptable because peer-support practices may differ across programmes and campuses.

 ## 1. Toolkit at a Glance

 ### Step-by-step summary
 1. A first-year student is connected to a peer mentor through an existing buddy or peer-support practice. (Programme coordinator / peer mentor)
 2. The peer mentor makes a low-threshold first contact. (Peer mentor)
 3. The mentor uses check-in prompts to discuss belonging, study routines, practical concerns and support awareness. (Peer mentor and student)
 4. The mentor helps the student identify one small next step. (Peer mentor and student)
 5. If the student needs more than peer support, the mentor guides the student to the right support route or contacts the student coach or programme coordinator. (Peer mentor / coordinator)
 6. The mentor records only a short reflection or, where needed, a factual referral cue. (Peer mentor / coordinator)

 ### Intended users
 Second-year or senior peer mentors or buddy actors, first-year students, student coaches, learning coaches, lecturers, programme coordinators or designated implementation leads, trajectory counsellors, language coaches and student support services involved in first-year student support at Odisee Brussels.

 ### When it is used
 During orientation, early first-year transition, preparation for first assessments, moments of reduced engagement, or any point where first-year students need approachable guidance before formal support is needed.

 ### This toolkit is not
 - a replacement for existing buddy or peer-support practices
 - a full new mentoring programme requiring major extra capacity
 - peer tutoring or course-content teaching
 - counselling, case management or crisis support
 - a monitoring or disciplinary system

 ## 2. Purpose, Scope and Safeguards

 ### Purpose
 To make peer support more consistent, approachable and connected to Odisee Brussels' first-year support pathway. Peer mentors provide a low-threshold first contact who helps students feel less alone, ask questions earlier and identify the right support route when needed.

 ### Scope
 Intended for a small pilot in one or two first-year programmes. Can be adapted to existing buddy systems, orientation activities, programme-level peer support or student support initiatives. Does not require new technology or a new staff role.

 ### Safeguards (summary)
 - Low-threshold support: approachable and informal, framed as support not assessment.
 - Role clarity: mentors are not lecturers, tutors, counsellors, case managers or crisis responders.
 - Student autonomy: students may accept, decline or pause support.
 - Boundaries and privacy: mentors should not collect sensitive personal information; referral notes should be short and factual.
 - Referral when needed: concerns beyond peer support passed to student coach or programme coordinator.
 - Feasible workload: short check-ins, ready prompts and simple templates.

 ## 3. Design Rationale: Evidence to Design

 The toolkit translates research on belonging, peer mentoring, help-seeking and first-year transition into practical tools. Core sources include Tinto (1975), Gopalan and Brady (2020), Crisp and Cruz (2009), Collings et al. (2014), Karabenick and Knapp (1991), Odisee (2026) and partner feedback.

 Key insights and design responses (summary):
 - Social and academic integration shapes persistence → peer mentoring positioned as social integration tool.
 - Sense of belonging linked to engagement and support use → includes check-ins and prompts focused on belonging.
 - Peer mentoring works when roles and support structures are clear → provides role card, check-in structure, boundaries and referral cues.
 - Students delay help-seeking when unsure where to go → includes message templates and prompts to move students toward appropriate support.

 ## 5. Roles and Boundaries

 Actors and responsibilities (summary):
 - First-year student/mentee: receives support; may decline disclosure.
 - Peer mentor: second-year/senior student; listens, shares experience, helps identify next steps and refer when needed. Not a tutor, counsellor, case manager or crisis responder.
 - Programme coordinator / student coach: adapts toolkit, prepares mentors, receives referral cues and connects to the right support actor.
 - Lecturers: may refer students to peer mentoring or share visible concern signals.
 - Student support services: provide specialised wellbeing, financial, practical support and learning/language coaching.

 Peer Mentor Role Card (summary):
 A peer mentor can welcome students; listen without judgement; share transition experience; help identify one small next step; encourage help-seeking; guide to the First-Year Self-Regulated Learning Guide; notice referral cues and contact student coach or coordinator when needed.

 A peer mentor should not teach course content, diagnose problems, act as counsellor, promise absolute confidentiality, handle urgent or crisis concerns alone, collect sensitive information, or replace professional staff.

 ## 6. Suggested Check-In Structure

 Suggested moments: first weeks; before major deadlines; after early feedback; mid-semester; before exams; after referral cue or student request.

 Five-step check-in flow:
 1. Welcome and personal check-in — "How have the last few weeks been for you?"
 2. What is coming up? — "What is coming up soon that feels important or difficult?"
 3. Main concern or question — "Is there one thing you would like to figure out today?"
 4. One small next step — "What is one small step you could take this week?"
 5. Confirm contact or support route — "Would it help to check in again, or should we look at who you could contact?"

 Aim: help student feel less alone, clarify next step, and connect to support when needed.

 ## 7. Conversation Prompts and Referral Cues

 Conversation prompts (examples):
 - Belonging: "Do you feel connected to people in your programme?"
 - Study routines: "How are you keeping track of deadlines?"
 - Academic adjustment: "Is anything about studying at Odisee different from what you expected?"
 - Support awareness: "Do you know where you would go if you needed academic or personal support?"
 - Practical pressures: "Are commuting, work, finances or other responsibilities making study routines harder?"
 - Confidence and help-seeking: "Is there a question you'd feel more comfortable asking a student than a lecturer?"
 - Next step: "What is one thing you can do before we check in again?"

 Referral cues and recommended responses (summary):
 - Ordinary first-year questions → continue peer support and identify next step.
 - Student unsure which service to contact → use First-Year Self-Regulated Learning Guide.
 - Repeated workload difficulty or confusion → encourage contact with learning coach; inform student coach/coordinator if repeated.
 - Repeated non-response or withdrawal → share concern cue with student coach/coordinator (do not classify independently).
 - Visible distress or serious wellbeing/financial/housing/safety concerns → do not handle alone; contact student coach, coordinator or student support services per internal procedures.
 - Student declines peer support → respect decision and share one support route for later use.

 Important boundary: mentors do not operate the Early Alert and Follow-Up Protocol independently.

 ## 8. Connection to the Other Deliverables

 Deliverable 1 (Peer Mentoring Toolkit): builds connection and low-threshold peer support.
 Deliverable 2 (Early Alert & Follow-Up Protocol): makes repeated or serious concern signals visible and guides warm follow-up — mentors contact student coach/coordinator when appropriate.
 Deliverable 3 (First-Year Self-Regulated Learning Guide): helps students build study routines and prepare help-seeking messages — mentors can use this to guide students.

 Simple decision logic: peer support question -> stay within Deliverable 1; study routine/help-seeking -> use Deliverable 3; repeated/combined concern cue -> contact student coach/coordinator and use Deliverable 2; urgent/specialised concern -> refer to student support services.

 ## 9. Ready-to-Use Templates

 **Template 1: First Check-In Message**
 Hi [Name],

 I am [Name], your peer mentor / buddy for the start of the year. I am a [second-year / senior] student in [programme]... Would you like a short check-in this week or next?

 **Template 2: Follow-Up After First Conversation**
 Hi [Name],

 Thanks for talking today. The small next step we agreed on was: [One small next step]...

 **Template 3: Support Navigation Message**
 Hi [Name],

 Based on what you mentioned, it may help to look at the First-Year Self-Regulated Learning Guide... If you want, we can look at the options together.

 **Template 4: Referral Cue Message to Designated Staff Contact**
 Student code/name, Date, Mentor name, Observed concern cue, Repeated/combined? Yes/No/Unsure, Student response, Support suggested, Reason for handover, Short factual note.

 **Template 5: Student Declines Peer Support**
 Hi [Name],

 Thanks for letting me know. That is completely okay. I will not keep following up about peer mentoring unless you want to use it later. If you need support, you can contact [support route].

 **Template 6: Peer Mentor Reflection Form (summary)**
 - Date of check-in
 - Conversation topics
 - Did student identify one small next step? Yes/No
 - Did student need support navigation information? Yes/No
 - Did any referral cue appear? Yes/No/Unsure
 - If yes, was student coach/coordinator informed? Yes/No/Not applicable
 - Did mentor stay within role boundaries? Yes/No/Unsure
 - What should be improved?

 ## 10. Pilot Use and Mentor Preparation

 Suggested pilot steps:
 1. Adapt the toolkit with local programme contacts and buddy practices.
 2. Select peer mentors (second-year/senior, approachable, reliable).
 3. Brief mentors on role boundaries, check-in structure, referral cues, privacy and Deliverables 2 & 3.
 4. Introduce toolkit to students as an optional support.
 5. Run check-ins using the suggested structure.
 6. Use referral cues when needed and pass concerns to student coach/coordinator.
 7. Collect feedback from students, mentors, coordinators and support actors.
 8. Refine the toolkit before wider use.

 Mentor preparation topics: purpose, role boundaries, check-in structure, conversation prompts, referral cues, connection to other deliverables, privacy and minimal notes.

 ## 11. Pilot Evaluation Indicators

 Evaluation focus and possible indicators (summary):
 - Student belonging and support clarity: short student feedback form.
 - Peer mentor confidence: peer mentor feedback form.
 - Usefulness of check-in structure: mentor reflection form / student feedback.
 - Referral boundary clarity: mentor feedback / coordinator review.
 - Connection to support navigation: mentor reflection form / student feedback.
 - Link to Early Alert Protocol: coordinator review.
 - Workload feasibility: staff and mentor feedback.
 - Improvement of toolkit: end-of-pilot review.

 ### End-of-Pilot Review
 Review anonymised feedback focusing on belonging, support approachability, boundary clarity and connection to the first-year support pathway. If pilot is feasible, refine and adapt for other programmes while keeping connection to existing buddy and student support structures.
