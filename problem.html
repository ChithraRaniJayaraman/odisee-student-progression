Problem Analysis

Understanding the challenges affecting student progression at Odisee

Current Context

Odisee, as an educational institution, faces various challenges in supporting student progression through their programmes. This analysis identifies key problem areas and contextual factors affecting student success.

Key Statistical Indicators

  • Student withdrawal rates - Identifying programmes with higher attrition
  • Progression delays - Students requiring additional time beyond standard duration
  • Performance disparities - Variation in outcomes across student demographics
  • Support service utilization - Gap between availability and actual usage

Identified Problem Areas

Academic Preparedness

Variations in entry-level competencies create challenges for equitable learning experiences and progression outcomes.

Support System Gaps

Limited early identification and targeted support for at-risk students during critical transition points.

Institutional Coordination

Siloed decision-making across departments reduces effectiveness of collaborative interventions.

Student Engagement

Insufficient connection between academic content and student motivations or career aspirations.

Data Infrastructure

Limited capacity for evidence-based planning and real-time identification of progression risks.

Resource Constraints

Competing demands on support services with limited resources for personalised interventions.

Root Cause Analysis

The problems identified above stem from interconnected systemic factors rather than isolated issues:

Structural Factors

  • Programme design that doesn't account for student diversity
  • Timing of support interventions (often reactive rather than proactive)
  • Limited feedback loops between institutional performance data and policy

Human Factors

  • Insufficient awareness among staff of progression challenges
  • Limited training in supports for diverse learner needs
  • Student awareness of available support services

Systemic Factors

  • Misalignment between teaching practices and learning needs
  • Insufficient coordination between academic and support services
  • External labour market pressures and shifting industry demands

Research Questions

This analysis leads to the following research questions guiding our investigation:

  1. What are the primary factors affecting student progression through Odisee programmes?
  2. How do these factors interact to create systemic patterns in student success?
  3. What evidence-based interventions can effectively address progression challenges?
  4. How can institutional systems be better coordinated to support student success?
  5. What metrics best indicate programme-level health and student progression effectiveness?
